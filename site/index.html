A private journal of your days.

Moment keeps what you write, feel, see, hear and where you were, in six separate logs. It is encrypted on your phone, with no account and no cloud.

Log anything

Six kinds of moment, each kept in its own place.

Nothing is merged into one mixed-up entry. A photo is a photo; a mood is a mood. Home shows today's of every kind together, and each kind has its own section.

Text

What is on your mind, in your own words.

Emoji

How you feel and how strongly, in one tap.

Photo

What you saw. Location data is stripped when you add it.

Video

How it moved, kept exactly as recorded.

Audio

A voice note, with a title and a note if you like.

Location

Where you were: a pin, a name and a note.

What it does

Small, calm, and complete.

Calendar

A month at a glance, with a dot for every kind of log on the day it happened. Days stay put when you travel.

Search

Full-text search as you type, across everything you have written, named or captioned. It runs on your phone.

Encrypted backup

One file holds everything, locked with a passphrase only you know. Restore is all or nothing, so a bad file changes nothing.

Export

Take your logs out as Markdown, JSON and the original files whenever you want. They are yours.

App lock

A PIN, with Face ID or fingerprint, in front of an encrypted database.

Trash

Deleted logs wait 30 days before they go for good, files included.

Light and dark

Follow the system or choose. Prefer solid surfaces to glass? One switch.

Built to be used by everyone

Every control is labelled for screen readers, tap targets are full size, and text fits at 200%.

Yours alone

Private by how it is built.

  • No account.
  • No cloud.
  • No analytics, no ads.
  • Encrypted on your phone.

The one exception, said plainly

Moment can use the internet for exactly one thing, and only if you turn it on.

  • Look up place names is off by default. While it is on, the coordinates of a new pin are sent to OpenStreetMap to suggest a name.
  • Nothing else in the app can open a connection. A test in the source keeps network code to one file and one address.
  • The reminder is a notification made on your phone, and it never shows anything from your journal.
  • You do not have to take our word for it: the source is open, and this page itself loads nothing from other servers.
